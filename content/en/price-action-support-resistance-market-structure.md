If your chart carries six indicators and no two agree, the problem is not which one to add: it is that you are watching derivatives of price instead of price. Every indicator lags, because it is computed from what has already happened. Price action starts from the only data that does not lag. Learning it takes less time than you lose each week changing settings.

## What price action is

It is reading the market through price movement alone: where it stopped, where it accelerated, where it turned back. Three elements cover most decisions: structure, levels, reaction.

## Structure: highs and lows

![Uptrend structure with higher highs and higher lows and a final break](/images/fig-price-action-en.svg)

### Uptrend

A sequence of higher highs and higher lows. While each pullback holds above the previous low, buyers are in control.

### Downtrend

Lower highs and lower lows. Each bounce fades below the previous high.

### Break of structure

In an uptrend, when price closes below the last higher low, the sequence is broken. That does not mean the trend has reversed: it means the bullish case is no longer confirmed and the risk of staying in has changed.

## Support and resistance: zones, not lines

Support is an area where demand halted declines; resistance is an area where supply halted advances. They are zones a few ticks or a few percent wide, not exact prices.

### Drawing the levels that matter

1. Start from the highest timeframe you use (daily or weekly).
2. Look for points where price reversed decisively, leaving a large move behind.
3. Give more weight to zones tested several times and to recent ones.
4. Draw few: if the chart is covered in lines, none is useful.

### Role reversal

Broken resistance tends to become support, and vice versa. A return to the level just broken — the retest — is one of the spots where risk/reward is most favourable, because the stop can sit just beyond the zone.

## Real breakout or false one?

False breakouts are common: price clears a level, draws in orders and turns back. Three filters reduce mistakes.

- **Close, not wick.** What counts is the candle closing beyond the level, not a brief poke through it.
- **Context.** A breakout in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend is more likely to follow through.
- **Confirmation.** Waiting for the retest costs part of the move but cuts bad entries.

No filter removes false breakouts. That is why the stop is not optional.

## From chart to trade

### A four-step procedure

1. **Context:** on the higher timeframe, is the trend up, down or sideways?
2. **Level:** is price near a relevant zone?
3. **Reaction:** on your trading timeframe, is price respecting or breaking that zone?
4. **Risk:** where is the stop that invalidates the idea, and does the distance to target offer at least twice the risk?

If even one answer is missing, there is no trade. Staying out is a position.

## The bottom line

Price action does not predict: it describes who is in control and where that control would be lost. Structure for direction, zones for location, closes for confirmation, stops for being wrong.
