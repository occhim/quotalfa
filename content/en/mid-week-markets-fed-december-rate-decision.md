The Federal Reserve's next decision is two weeks away, but markets have largely made up their minds already. Futures pricing points to a rate increase on December 9, and today brings the last major US inflation reading before the meeting. When expectations are this one-sided, the risk is no longer the decision itself: it is the gap between what is priced in and what actually happens.

This mid-week note covers the verified facts, what to watch today, and how to handle position risk around a central bank event. It is educational, not a forecast.

## Where the Fed stands

Here is the policy path so far this autumn, taken from the Fed's own statements:

| Meeting | Decision | Federal funds target range | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 15-16, 2026 | Raised by 0.25 points | 3.75% to 4.00% | 12-0 |
| October 27-28, 2026 | Held | 3.75% to 4.00% | 10-2 |
| December 8-9, 2026 | Pending | - | - |

The October hold was not unanimous. Two members, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, dissented in favour of another 0.25-point increase. The statement described inflation as "elevated" and the labour market as broadly stable.

### What the October minutes revealed

The minutes, released on November 18, 2026, are more hawkish than the hold suggests. According to the Fed's published text:

- **Almost all** participants said further increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlook to bring inflation back to 2%.
- **A couple** said a move larger than 25 basis points could be considered in December if inflation data were unfavourable.
- **A few** who supported holding called it a close call.
- **The vast majority** saw upside risks to inflation.
- Staff estimates put headline PCE inflation at 3.6% and core PCE at 3.1% over the 12 months to September.

No participant discussed rate cuts. That is the key context for everything that follows.

## What the market is pricing

According to a Reuters report dated November 25, 2026, the CME FedWatch tool, which derives probabilities from fed funds futures, showed a **97% probability** of a December hike and a 66% probability of a further increase in January.

A 97% probability does not mean the decision is certain. It means that a 0.25-point hike, by itself, should carry little new information. What moves prices on decision day is usually:

1. **Surprises in size**: a larger or smaller move than priced.
2. **Guidance**: the statement wording and the press conference tone on what comes next.
3. **Projections**: December is a Summary of Economic Projections meeting, so the updated rate path ("dot plot") will be published alongside the decision.

## What to watch today

Reuters lists two US releases for Wednesday, November 25:

- **October PCE price index**: consensus expects a 0.3% monthly rise, after 0.5% the month before.
- **Q3 GDP, second estimate**: consensus expects 3.5% annualised growth.

PCE is the inflation measure the Fed targets, which makes it the most relevant data point before the meeting. A reading well above consensus would revive the "more than 25 basis points" scenario mentioned in the minutes. A softer reading would mostly reinforce what is already priced. Check the official release and timing on the issuing agency's website, as calendars can shift. ### Why it matters outside the US

Fed decisions affect global markets through the US dollar, bond yields and risk appetite. Reuters reported the dollar index flat at 103.85 and EUR/USD at 1.0961 this morning. For investors and traders in any country, the transmission channel is the same: tighter US policy tends to influence currency moves, borrowing costs and equity valuations elsewhere. How much it affects your own situation depends on your currency, your assets and your local central bank.

## Managing risk around a central bank event

Event days often bring wider spreads, faster moves and gaps. You cannot control the outcome, but you can control your exposure. The core tool is position sizing:

**Position size = Amount at risk / (Stop distance × Value per point)**

Example with a €10,000 account risking 1% per trade, so €100:

- Normal conditions, stop 40 points away, €1 per point per unit: 100 / (40 × 1) = **2.5 units**.
- Event conditions, stop widened to 80 points to absorb volatility: 100 / (80 × 1) = **1.25 units**.

The euro risk stays the same; the size halves. That is the trade-off you accept when you widen a stop for an event. Other options include reducing size further, staying flat into the release, or waiting for the first reaction to settle. Remember that a stop does not guarantee your exit price when the market gaps.

If you trade CFDs, forex or any leveraged product, keep in mind that most retail accounts lose money, and leverage amplifies losses as much as gains.

## The bottom line

The Fed has hiked once this autumn, held in October with two dissents in favour of more, and the minutes show near-unanimous support for further tightening. Markets price a December hike almost fully, so the information is in the size, the guidance and the new projections, not in the headline move. Today's PCE reading is the last big input. Whatever happens, decide your exposure before the release, not during it.
