The Federal Reserve meets on December 8-9, and this was the week that delivered the last major US inflation reading before the decision. The numbers came in hot, futures markets kept pricing a rate increase, and yet US stocks posted their strongest week in more than a year. That combination is a useful reminder that markets react to surprises against expectations, not to whether the news sounds good or bad.

This recap covers what was verified this week, what is on the calendar next, and how to think about risk when the schedule is this dense. It is educational and contains no forecasts.

## What happened this week

### US inflation: the Fed's preferred gauge accelerated

The Bureau of Economic Analysis published the Personal Income and Outlays report for October on Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (figures as reported that day by ABC News and StockAnalysis):

| Measure | October 2026 | September 2026 | Consensus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline PCE, year on year | 3.8% | 3.6% | 3.8% |
| Headline PCE, month on month | 0.3% | 0.5% | - |
| Core PCE, year on year | 3.3% | 3.1% | 3.2% |
| Core PCE, month on month | 0.3% | 0.3% | - |

Headline inflation matched expectations, but core inflation, which strips out food and energy, came in one tenth above the forecast. Personal income rose 0.7% against a 0.4% forecast, partly because of one-off rebate payments, while spending rose 0.3% in nominal terms and was flat after adjusting for prices.

### The Fed: a hike is almost fully priced

According to CME Group's FedWatch tool, as cited by ABC News on November 25, futures implied roughly a 97% probability of a 0.25-point increase on December 9. The federal funds target range currently stands at 3.75% to 4.00% after the September hike and the October hold. We covered the October minutes and the policy path in our [mid-week note on the December decision](/en/blog/mid-week-markets-fed-december-rate-decision).

When a move is close to fully priced, the decision itself carries little new information. What can still surprise is the size of the move, the wording of the statement and the new economic projections, which are published at this meeting.

### Stocks: a sharp rebound in a short week

US markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. According to the Associated Press (November 27, 2026, updated during Friday's session), the S&P 500 was heading for a weekly gain of about 5.6%, its biggest since April 2025, after volatile swings in large technology stocks on Tuesday and Wednesday. These are figures reported during a shortened session, not final closes.

## How to read the numbers

A release moves prices when it differs from what was expected. A simple way to frame it:

**Surprise = Actual - Consensus**

If payrolls print 150K against a 90K consensus, the surprise is +60K; at 40K it is -50K. The same headline number can be bullish or bearish depending on the estimate and on revisions to previous months, so always read the prior-month revision as well.

## Managing risk in a heavy week

Event days often bring wider spreads, slippage and fast moves. The arithmetic of position sizing does not change, but the inputs do:

**Position size = (Account × Risk %) / (Stop distance × Value per point)**

With a €10,000 account and 1% risk, the maximum loss is €100. With a 50-point stop at €1 per point, that is 2 units. If you widen the stop to 100 points to allow for event volatility, the size must halve to 1 unit to keep the same €100 risk.

If you trade CFDs, forex or any leveraged product, remember that most retail accounts lose money with these instruments, and gaps around releases can move price beyond your stop.
