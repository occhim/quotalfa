Waiting for the “right moment” to invest is the costliest choice you can make, because that moment is only recognisable afterwards. Each year of delay does not remove a year of contributions: it removes the final year of compounding, the one worth most. A savings plan solves the problem at the root by removing the decision.

## What a savings plan is

A savings plan, also known as dollar-cost averaging, means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals in the same instrument. You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high, so your average entry price evens out over time.

### Why ETFs

An ETF tracks an index at low cost. A single global equity ETF gives you thousands of companies in dozens of countries. Diversification, transparency and low costs are the three conditions that, over the long run, matter more than picking individual stocks.

## The numbers: €200 a month for 20 years

![Value of a €200 monthly plan over 20 years at different hypothetical returns](/images/fig-etf-savings-plan-en.svg)

Over twenty years you pay in €48,000. At a constant hypothetical annual return of 4% the plan would be worth about €73,400; at 6%, about €92,400. These are simulations before costs, taxes and inflation, and no market returns a constant rate: they show the order of magnitude, not a forecast.

### The cost of waiting

Same contribution, same 6%, but for ten years instead of twenty: the result is about €32,800. Halve the time and the final capital does not halve: it falls to just over a third. Compound interest does most of its work in the last years.

## Setting it up in four steps

1. **Finish your emergency fund.** A plan only works if you are never forced to stop it.
2. **Choose the index.** For a long horizon, a global equity index is the most common starting point. The bond share depends on your horizon and tolerance for swings.
3. **Choose the ETF.** Look at the annual cost (TER), fund size, dividend policy (accumulating or distributing) and where the fund is domiciled; if you invest from Europe, check it is UCITS-compliant.
4. **Automate.** A recurring purchase on the same date each month. Then stop checking it daily.

### Accumulating or distributing

Accumulating ETFs reinvest dividends; distributing ETFs pay them into your account. In many countries the former defer tax until you sell, but not everywhere: check how your jurisdiction treats fund income before you choose.

## Costs and tax

### Costs

Three items count: the ETF’s TER, the broker’s commission on each purchase and the bid–ask spread. On a €200 plan, a flat fee of a few euros per order weighs heavily: check whether your broker offers reduced-fee recurring plans.

### Tax

Tax treatment depends on where you live: capital gains rates, dividend taxation, wealth taxes and whether losses can be offset all vary between jurisdictions. Before you start, check three things: how gains are taxed when you sell, how dividends are taxed, and who calculates the tax, your broker or you. If your country offers tax-advantaged investment accounts, look at those before an ordinary one.

## The mistake that undoes the plan

Stopping when markets fall. That is when the same amount buys more units, so it is when the plan works best. If a 30% drop would make you quit, lower the equity share before you start, not during.

## The bottom line

An ETF savings plan replaces forecasts with a rule: same amount, same date, for many years. It does not remove market risk, but it removes the most common error, which is timing.
