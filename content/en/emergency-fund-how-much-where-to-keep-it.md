A boiler to replace, a car stuck at the garage, two months without a salary. None of these is rare, and each can force you to sell investments at the worst moment or borrow at consumer-credit rates. If you cannot say precisely how many months you could live with no income, this is the first thing to fix. Before ETFs, before trading, before everything.

## What an emergency fund is (and is not)

It is a cash reserve for genuine shocks only: job loss, medical bills, repairs that cannot wait. It is not an investment and it does not need to earn a return. Its job is to be there, in full, on the day you need it.

### Why it comes before any investment

Investing without a buffer makes you a forced seller in waiting. Markets often fall exactly when the economy slows and incomes are at risk: with no cash you would have to sell at a loss what you should have held.

## How much to set aside

Start from expenses, not salary.

1. Add up one month of essential outgoings: housing, utilities, food, transport, loan payments, insurance.
2. Multiply by the number of months that fits your profile.

![Months of expenses to hold by income stability](/images/fig-emergency-fund-en.svg)

### Three profiles

- **Two stable household incomes:** 3 months may be enough.
- **A single income, or dependants:** at least 6 months.
- **Variable income (self-employed, seasonal, commission):** 9 to 12 months.

With essential expenses of €1,800 a month and one income, the target is €10,800. If that feels out of reach, set a first milestone of one month of expenses: it is already the difference between a setback and a debt.

## Where to keep it

Three requirements, in this order: safety, access, return.

### Suitable homes

- **An easy-access savings or deposit account:** it earns something and returns the money within days. Read the withdrawal terms: early access can cost you accrued interest.
- **An interest-bearing current account:** instant access.
- **A slice in your main account:** about one month, for same-day emergencies.

Check that the bank belongs to a deposit guarantee scheme: in the European Union, deposits are protected up to €100,000 per depositor per bank; outside the EU, limits and rules vary by country. Also check how interest is taxed where you live and whether any account charges apply: they affect your net return, not your safety.

### Where not to keep it

Shares, equity ETFs, crypto, long-dated bonds. They can be down 20% in the very month you need the money. Fixed-term accounts with no early access are unsuitable too: the extra yield does not make up for being locked out.

## How to build it without noticing

### Automate

Set a standing transfer for the day after payday, into an account separate from your spending one. What you do not see, you do not spend.

### Refill it after use

If you draw on it, the first financial goal of the following months is to bring it back to its starting level. Investments can wait.

## The bottom line

An emergency fund does not make money: it prevents losses. Work out essential expenses, multiply by the months for your profile, and keep the sum liquid and protected. Only then does it make sense to talk about return.
