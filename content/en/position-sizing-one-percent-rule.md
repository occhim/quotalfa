Ten losing trades in a row happen to every system, including the profitable ones. What separates traders who survive from those who blow up is not the entry: it is the quantity bought. If you size positions by feel today, you are leaving to chance the one variable that decides how long you last. It takes five minutes to fix, before your next order.

## What position sizing is

Position sizing is working out how many units to trade so that, if the stop loss is hit, the loss is exactly what you decided in advance. It does not start from the capital you “feel like” investing. It starts from risk.

### The 1% rule

On any trade you risk at most 1% of capital. With €10,000 that means accepting a €100 loss, not investing €100. The position itself can be worth far more: what matters is the distance between entry and stop.

## The formula, step by step

**Units = (Capital × Risk %) ÷ (Entry price − Stop price)**

1. Set the money at risk: capital × percentage.
2. Place the stop at a technical level that invalidates the idea.
3. Measure risk per unit: entry minus stop.
4. Divide the first number by the third.

### Worked example

- Capital: €10,000
- Risk per trade: 1% → €100
- Entry at €50, stop at €48 → €2 risk per share
- Units: 100 ÷ 2 = **50 shares**, a position worth €2,500

With the stop at €49, risk per share falls to €1 and you could buy 100. Tight stop, larger position; wide stop, smaller position. The maximum loss is €100 either way.

## Why 1%

Because losses compound, and recovery is asymmetric.

![Drawdown after ten straight losses by risk per trade](/images/fig-position-sizing-en.svg)

| Risk per trade | Capital lost after 10 losses | Gain needed to get back to even |
|---|---|---|
| 1% | 9.6% | 10.6% |
| 2% | 18.3% | 22.4% |
| 5% | 40.1% | 67.0% |
| 10% | 65.1% | 186.8% |

At 1% a bad run is a nuisance. At 10% it is the end: to recover you must nearly triple what is left.

### The asymmetry of recovery

Lose 20% and you need 25% to get back to even. Lose 50% and you need 100%. The deeper the drawdown, the more recovery demands returns no system produces consistently. Capping the loss is not caution. It is arithmetic.

## Three mistakes that cancel the rule

### Moving the stop after entry

Widen the stop on an open position and your risk is no longer 1%. The stop is decided beforehand and only ever moved in your favour.

### Ignoring correlation

Five 1% positions in the same sector are not five independent risks: they are one 5% bet. Set a ceiling on total open risk as well, for example 3%.

### Forgetting costs and gaps

Commissions, spreads and gap openings can make the real loss larger than the calculated one. On instruments that close overnight or over the weekend, size down.

## The bottom line

Position sizing turns trading from a series of bets into a repeatable process: choose the risk, find the stop, and the quantity falls out of a division. It will not make you win more often, but it ensures you are still trading when your system starts working again.
